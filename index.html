Abstract

Satisfy a deployment-time constraint without leaving the policy

A flow-matching policy deployed in the field must often satisfy a constraint that was absent from its demonstrations, such as a collision-free path or a particular grasp orientation. Existing training-free samplers either guide the denoising trajectory with a constraint gradient at noisy intermediates, where a hard constraint gives no usable gradient, or project the finished sample onto the feasible set and execute it, which yields an action the policy would never draw.

Our key insight is that the projection is a cheap, clean, feasible signal, and the only mistake is executing it. CS-Pi draws from the policy, projects the clean sample to the nearest feasible point, and resamples from the flow model biased toward that point rather than executing it. Iterating drives the samples toward constraint satisfaction, with no noisy-state gradient, no auxiliary network, and no per-deployment tuning; it extends to arbitrary costs with just a change to the projection. Across a 2D diagnostic, a 7-DoF shelf pick-and-place, and a zero-shot Franka deployment, CS-Pi is the only sampler that stays in-distribution wherever the constraint permits, departing from the policy only as far as the constraint forces.

The idea

Four ways to enforce a constraint, one that keeps the policy

Guidance steers with a proxy gradient and drifts off-manifold. Projection is feasible but off-distribution. Rejection is in-distribution but slow. CS-Pi runs the unmodified flow, projects, and renoises to a depth set by the violation, so every draw stays in-distribution while iterating to feasibility.

Method

Draw, project, renoise to n = k/(k+δ), re-run

The renoising depth is the whole algorithm: a large violation triggers deep renoising so the next draw can reach a different policy mode (a different route around the obstacle); a small violation triggers shallow renoising that refines the current mode. The depth n = k/(k+δ) follows directly from the violation δ.
